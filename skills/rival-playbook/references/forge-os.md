The forge tier's runtime layer for rival-playbook (the 33rd runtime

## 1. The thinking framework — decision rules

* **Both sides, same axes (知彼知己).** The census's one law: both
  sides graded on the SAME public axes, every row artifact-cited
  and date-pinned. A census that grades the rival on artifacts and
  ourselves on intentions is not a census — the corpus is graded by
  its own gates, never by its own README.
* **The grade is the gate, not the README.** M1's law: grade =
  what the artifact enforces, never what the README claims. A
  "validated" with no validator script is a census row showing the
  ABSENCE of a validator — intent and enforcement are different
  rows, and only one of them counts.
* **Hollows, not fronts (避实击虚 / 围魏救赵).** The only axes worth
  contesting are the ones with no artifact-backed answer on the
  other side; on a contested front, strike the dependency their
  effort rests on, not the position they defend — the supply line,
  not the front line.
* **Concentration to two (压强原则).** At most two hollows receive
  force; ownership is demonstrated by GATES — validator runs, test
  counts, auditor pass rates — mechanical enforcement the census can
  point at. Spreading force across five axes owns none of them.
* **Win first, then seek battle (先胜而后求战).** Gates before
  claims: the campaign is won in the wiring commit, and the market
  claim ships only after the numbers exist — the hype war is
  refused, not fought harder.
* **Speed is refresh (兵贵神速).** The census rides the release
  train, not the calendar; a row older than 180 days is STALE and
  the map says so. Staleness is stamped, never ignored.
* **Retirement without resentment (兵无常势 / 走为上).** A hollow
  dies the day the rival ships a disciplined answer; the retirement
  is recorded as the method working. Abandoning an axis the rival
  owns with discipline is the pressure principle's corollary, not
  a defeat.
* **No un-citable moves (the mechanical ban).** A move whose
  execution requires an un-citable claim about the rival is not a
  move — the deception stratagem family fails the census gate by
  construction (stratagem-map's banned register, hard rule 3).

### The opening ladder — where the campaign's hollows come from

The rules above govern the campaign once the census is committed;
the ladder produces the census FIRST — 知彼知己's own opening move
(know the rival from artifacts BEFORE any verdict) made runnable as
questions. Every rung is an artifact-reading question that writes
census rows — no rung grades, maps hollows, allocates force, or
ships claims; R2's hollow verdicts stay behind the ladder and
consume its rows.

| # | The question | What it hunts | Grounding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | What does the rival's charter/README claim its corpus does? | the doctrine rows: their own words, artifact-cited and date-pinned — intent, never enforcement | R1's charter/README artifact class (SKILL.md §3 R1; M1's census table) |
| G2 | What does their validator actually gate? | the authoring-gate rows: the enforcement their validator runs, set against the intent G1 recorded — the first absence candidates | R1's validator class; the house's own validator is the calibration instrument |
| G3 | What do their tests count — and what do the counts assert? | the enforcement-count rows: suite size plus assertion class (membership guards vs cosmetic smoke) | R1's test-suite class — the census grades enforcement, not presence |
| G4 | Which of their organs carry a method layer? | the method-layer rows: per-organ critique-and-upgrade modules — the A16-class absence candidates | R1's method-layer class (the axis the census grades both sides on) |
| G5 | What closes their loop — auditor, report gate, benchmark? | the closure rows: what mechanically keeps unverified claims from shipping on their side | R1's auditor and benchmark classes (M1's census table) |
| G6 | Where is the record silent — which axes have no artifact on either side? | the absence rows: hollow candidates (no artifact on their side) distinguished from build-first rows (no artifact on EITHER side) | R2's hollow rule — a hollow verdict cites the census rows that show the absence |

Ranking rationale: hollow priority = our gate-side strength on the
axis × their artifact absence × the season's segment yield. An axis
we enforce mechanically while they cover it with intent only (G1
rows with no G2 enforcement rows) is the cheapest ownership and
ranks first; an axis absent on both sides is a build-first row — it
feeds the pressure allocation, not the hollow map; an axis the
rival owns with discipline is retirement (走为上) — contesting
owned ground is waste. Rows out; no rung graded, no verdict
drafted.

Generation falsifier: a rival census that yields zero absence rows
means the reader graded the README, not the artifacts — intent read
as enforcement, the census's one law violated. The artifact classes
are re-read (validator tree, test tree, per-organ method modules)
before any hollow map ships. A census whose rows are all
doctrine-class (G1) with no enforcement rows is the same failure
in a different hat. And a hollow verdict that cites zero census
rows is not a verdict — it is a mood with a Chinese accent; hard
rule 3 applies to strategy exactly as it applies to findings.

#### The ladder's payload kit — the census rows (customize before use)

Generation writes rows; these are the row classes the rungs name,
every one a read of the rival's public artifacts (no rung grades,
maps, allocates, or claims; the verdict mechanics live in R1–R5
and the season layer's tables):

| Read row | The read payload class | Writes which row |
| --- | --- | --- |
| doctrine row | the rival's own charter/README claims, quoted and date-pinned | intent row (G1) |
| gate row | validator presence + what it mechanically enforces | enforcement row (G2) |
| count row | test suite size + the assertion classes the tests actually run | enforcement row (G3) |
| method row | per-organ method-layer presence on their side | A16-absence row (G4) |
| closure row | auditor/benchmark presence + their pass discipline | closure row (G5) |
| absence row | the axis × rival empty cells, both sides named | hollow-candidate / build-first row (G6) |
| stale row | rows past the staleness window, stamped with their age | refresh trigger (M5's release-train loop) |

Customization knobs, stated in the plan note: the staleness window
(the 180-day default vs a rival on a faster release cadence), the
axis depth (which artifact classes get rows — the six-class default
or the pressure-relevant subset), and the allocation cap (two
hollows by default vs one under a thin season's budget).

## 2. The behavioral protocol — situation, move, refusal

* **R1 — a rival is named** (or positioning is questioned).
  Move: the reading ladder, G1 first, census rows to the dossier —
  both sides, same axes, dates pinned.
  Refusal: characterizing the rival beyond its own artifacts —
  no disparagement, no FUD, no speculation about internals or
  motives (hard rule 2).
* **R2 — a claim about the rival (or ourselves) is about to ship.**
  Move: the duel rule — find the measured axis, cap the wording at
  it, attach the census pins.
  Refusal: "best", "most advanced", "industry-leading" with no
  measured axis behind them (hard rule 4 — the banned list is
  mechanical).
* **R3 — the rival releases.**
  Move: re-census the release's affected artifact classes, stamp
  the stale rows, retire the hollows it answers with corrections
  on record.
  Refusal: keeping a retired hollow on the map because the
  allocation loved it — the map dies the day the market moves.
* **R4 — an un-citable move is proposed** (seed a narrative, make
  them look bad, whisper campaigns).
  Move: none. Record the proposal as banned-class (the deception
  register) and re-point the allocation at an evidence move.
  Refusal: the deception family is rejected by construction — a
  move that requires an un-citable claim is not a move this corpus
  can run.
* **R5 — the allocation is pressured to spread** (a third hollow
  "while we're at it").
  Move: hold the cap — at most two; the season layer's Tian Ji
  table already conceded the unwinnable segment on record.
  Refusal: five thin axes own nothing — the pressure arithmetic
  is the rule, not a preference.
* **R6 — the season review.**
  Move: the ledger summary — census rows counted, hollows held
  and retired, claims shipped with their caps — feeding the next
  allocation (campaign-protocol's REVIEW format).
  Refusal: closing a season with an unmeasured assertion parked as
  if it were measured — the exit gate demands numbers stated.

## 3. The scripting arsenal — techniques as runnable craft

All three tools are read-only, operator-side census arithmetic.
None of them touches the rival's systems — the census reads public
artifacts the way subdomain-hunter reads certificate logs.

### T1 — the staleness stamper

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# T1: stamp census staleness. Reads a census JSON of rows (axis,
# side, artifact, pin date in ISO form); a row older than the
# window (default 180 days — the W4 floor) is stamped STALE and
# becomes a re-census trigger. Ages computed in code, never by
# eye. Read-only.

import json, sys
from datetime import date

def parse_day(d):
    return date(*map(int, d.split("-")))

def check(rows, window, today):
    stale = []
    for row in rows:
        age = (today - parse_day(row["pin"])).days
        if age > window:
            stale.append((row.get("axis", "?"), row.get("side", "?"),
                          row["pin"], age))
    return stale

if __name__ == "__main__":
    census = json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))
    window = int(sys.argv[2]) if len(sys.argv) > 2 else 180
    stale = check(census.get("rows", []), window, date.today())
    print("staleness check: %d STALE row(s) at window=%dd"
          % (len(stale), window))
    for axis, side, pin, age in stale:
        print("  STALE: %-24s %-4s pinned %s (%dd old)"
              % (axis, side, pin, age))
    print("staleness is stamped, never ignored (M5's loop)")
```

### T2 — the hollow-verdict validator

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# T2: validate the hollow map's citations. Reads a JSON file of
# verdict rows (axis, verdict, cites); a hollow verdict citing zero
# census rows is REJECTED (no hollow without rows — M2's floor),
# and so is an owned verdict with no artifact citation. Read-only.

import json, sys

VALID = {"hollow", "owned", "build_first", "retired"}

def check(verdicts):
    failures = []
    for v in verdicts:
        verdict, cites = v.get("verdict", "?"), v.get("cites", [])
        if verdict not in VALID:
            failures.append((v.get("axis", "?"),
                             "unknown verdict %r" % verdict))
        elif verdict == "hollow" and not cites:
            failures.append((v.get("axis", "?"),
                             "hollow without census rows"))
        elif verdict == "owned" and not cites:
            failures.append((v.get("axis", "?"),
                             "owned without artifact citation"))
    return failures

if __name__ == "__main__":
    doc = json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))
    failures = check(doc.get("verdicts", []))
    if failures:
        print("map check: %d REJECTED verdict(s)" % len(failures))
        for axis, why in failures:
            print("  REJECT: %-24s %s" % (axis, why))
        print("no hollow without rows — a verdict citing nothing "
              "is a mood, not a map")
    else:
        print("map check: OK — every verdict carries its citations")
```

### T3 — the wording-cap checker

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# T3: enforce the duel rule's wording cap at ship time. Reads a
# claims JSON (claim text + optional measured-axis pin); the
# banned-superlative list is mechanical (M4's rule): any
# superlative in a claim without an axis pin returns the claim
# for capping. Read-only.

import json, sys

BANNED = ("best", "most advanced", "industry-leading",
          "world-class", "state-of-the-art", "unmatched")

def check(claims):
    failures = []
    for claim in claims:
        text = " ".join(claim.get("text", "").lower().split())
        for word in BANNED:
            if word in text and not claim.get("axis_pin"):
                failures.append((claim.get("text", "?"), word))
    return failures

if __name__ == "__main__":
    doc = json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))
    failures = check(doc.get("claims", []))
    if failures:
        print("wording cap: %d claim(s) returned" % len(failures))
        for text, word in failures:
            print("  CAP: %-48s (%r without a measured axis)"
                  % (text, word))
        print("wording is capped at the demonstrated axis — the "
              "measured form is what ships")
    else:
        print("wording cap: OK — no superlative without its pin")
```

## 4. The rigid run-order — step by step

* **S1 — the census reading.** The ladder, G1–G6, rows on the
  dossier, both sides on the same axes (this file's §1).
* **S2 — the staleness stamp and the map.** T1 stamps the window;
  the hollow verdicts cite rows; T2 validates every verdict.
* **S3 — the allocation.** At most two hollows, through the season
  layer's comparative tables (campaign-protocol.md S3).
* **S4 — the duel.** Any claim ships through the wording cap (T3)
  with its measured axis and census pins attached.
* **S5 — the release train.** Re-census on every rival release;
  retirements and downgrades land as corrections on record.
* **S6 — the season review.** The REVIEW ledger closes the season
  and feeds the next allocation (campaign-protocol.md S6).

## 5. Falsifiers — what would prove this runtime wrong

* A census whose rows are all doctrine-class and zero
  enforcement-class → intent was read as enforcement; the map is
  void until the artifact classes are re-read.
* A hollow verdict that survives T2 with zero citations → the
  validator failed or was bypassed; nothing ships until repaired.
* A season holding three committed segments → the cap was broken;
  by the pressure arithmetic, one of them is thin by construction.
* A claim that ships with an uncapped superlative → the duel rule
  failed at ship time; the correction is the capped form, on
  record.
