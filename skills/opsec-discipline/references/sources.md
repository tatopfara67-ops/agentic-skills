## 2. The psych-read — documented behavior, per lens

* **L1 heuristics** (repeating shortcuts, ≥ 3 independent
  artifacts each):
  * Price-the-trail economics — the 2005 principle ("data is
    evidence"), the 2013 interview ("reduce the quantity and the
    quality of information available to the adversary"), the
    2013 "Observations" post ("OPSEC comes at a cost... part of
    that cost is efficiency").
  * Post-mortem as the primary teaching instrument — the
    LulzSec violations slide-by-slide (2012 deck), the DPR
    contamination chain (2013-10-09), the Kim five-phase
    analysis (2013-12-21), the Hezbollah rollback (2013-03-12),
    the standing instruction "read court cases, see where the
    errors were made" (2014 deck notes).
  * Compartmentation before tools — "Ignorance Is Strength"
    (2013), the interview ("Security comes from the way that you
    live your life, not the tools"), the COMSEC conclusion
    ("The ONLY thing that can save you from tool failure is
    Compartmentation").
  * Aphoristic compression as a memory technology — "STFU"
    (2012), "No logs. No crime." (2012), "OPSEC will get you
    through times of no crypto better than crypto will get you
    through times of no OPSEC" (2013), "If you hack, don't
    speak..." (2012), "you can't win" (2014).
* **L2 obsession**: counterintelligence applied to computing —
  one problem class across 24 years: the operator's visibility
  to the adversary. Anti-forensics (2002 Phrack, 2003/2005
  Black Hat) → OPSEC tradecraft (2012–2014: the deck, the blog
  cycle, COMSEC) → APT organizational analysis (2017–2018) →
  cyberwar and info-op analysis (2022–2026, newsletter and
  Between Two Nerds). The 2013 interview states it as the
  program: "extract [clandestine tradecraft], inject with
  hacker growth hormone, and then teach."
* **L3 aesthetics** (the figure's own words, cited): tools as
  weights — "Expecting the tools to provide security for you is
  like buying a set of weights and then sitting around waiting
  for your fitness to improve" (2013); the anti-fetish stance —
  "'Open Source' as a measure of quality is bullshit" (2014
  notes); the appreciation of correct design — Pond praised for
  its threat model, not its magic (2014); the tradecraft
  literary canon quoted throughout (Biggie's ten rules, The
  Wire's Avon Barksdale and Stringer Bell, Dulles' 73 Rules) —
  an aesthetic that treats espionage literature as engineering
  literature.
* **L4 failure mode**: epistemic honesty about source quality —
  the "Anonymity Is Hard" disclaimer ("This information is
  based on newspaper articles, so it is of limited accuracy"),
  the DPR post marked as "an abridged version" pointing to the
  fuller timeline, the COMSEC presenters' note ("they're not
  all things we actually said during the presentation"). No
  public retractions of his own method claims were needed on
  record; corrections arrive as follow-up posts (the detection
  posts' "update jujur" pattern is house-side, the grugq-side
  pattern is the abridged/fuller pairing).
* **L5 cadence**: post-mortems ship fast after the events they
  price — LulzSec arrests (2012) → the Ekoparty 2012 deck with
  the violation slides; DPR arrest (2013-10-01) → the post
  2013-10-09; Harvard bomb hoax (2013-12-16) → the post
  2013-12-21. The teaching corpus clusters 2012–2014 (the peak
  OPSEC era); the later corpus shifts to analysis and keynotes,
  and the 2022 keynote re-serves the 2012 deck — the doctrine
  outliving its own era, which is itself cadence evidence.
